# Downstream Hypotension Prediction

## Overview

All downstream models predict hypotension events from 30-minute waveform windows.
A positive label means a hypotension event (5 consecutive minutes with MAP < 65)
begins within a 5-minute forecast horizon after the window ends.

## Data Split

All models use the same fixed subject-level split
(`manifests/hypotension_subject_split_fixed_v1.csv`):

| Split | Subjects | ICU Stays | Samples | Positive Events | Prevalence |
|-------|----------|-----------|---------|-----------------|------------|
| Train (folds 2–9) | 2,013 | 3,195 | 1,022,563 | 43,950 | 4.3% |
| Val (fold 1) | 255 | 399 | 127,831 | 5,493 | 4.3% |
| Test (fold 0) | 256 | 407 | 127,811 | 5,494 | 4.3% |

Algorithm: corrected stratified group 10-fold (seed=16), enforcing subject-level
disjointness. All models share the same sample caches derived from this split,
ensuring direct comparability.

### Sample Generation Asymmetry

The sample cache is generated by `calculate_samples_forecast` (upstream design):

- **Positive stays**: Each 30-minute window is placed so its end is exactly
  `forecast_window_sec` (300 s = 5 min) before event onset. Only hypotension
  events trigger window placement — no windows are generated for the quiet
  periods between events. This produces irregularly spaced windows with gaps
  ranging from 6 minutes (back-to-back events) to 100+ hours (long inter-event
  intervals).
- **Negative stays**: Every minute of the stay is listed as a candidate window
  start, producing dense nearly-contiguous coverage with ~60-second steps
  (~1750 windows per typical stay).

**Quantitative consequence**: Negative stays contribute ~1750 windows each,
while positive stays contribute only as many windows as they have discrete
hypotension events (often fewer than 300 despite spanning days or weeks of
recording). The 4.3% class prevalence is therefore partially an artifact of the
asymmetric sampling density — positive patients are heavily undersampled
relative to their total recording time.

**Visualization implication**: Trajectory and embedding visualizations of
positive stays show irregularly sampled snapshots clustered around events, not
a continuous physiological time series. Gaps in the embedding trajectory
correspond to unsampled inter-event periods, not missing data.

This means:
1. Positive stays are sparsely sampled (only event-adjacent windows exist)
2. The model never sees "normal" physiology from a patient who later becomes
   hypotensive — all positive-stay windows are pre-crisis
3. Cross-patient embedding comparisons are confounded by different temporal
   sampling densities

## Model Details

### Supervised PatchTST (full fine-tuning)

- **Architecture**: PatchTST encoder (3 layers, 8 heads, d=512) + attentive
  classifier head
- **Training**: Full fine-tuning from random initialization
- **Result**: AUROC 0.8688, AP 0.2766
- **Config**: `jobs/baselines/configs/supervised_patchtst_hypotension_full.yaml`

### Multi-scale PatchTST

Hierarchical dual-path tokenizer adding fine-grained 200ms local encoder
(beat-level) fused with the existing 1-second coarse tokenizer via a learnable
gate.

- **Coarse path**: `kernel_size=125, stride=125` (1s patches → 1,800 tokens)
- **Fine path**: `kernel_size=25, stride=25` (200ms patches → 5 tokens per
  coarse group). Local transformer attends within each group.
- **Fusion**: Learnable gate combining coarse and fine representations
- **Additional params**: +275K (+2.9% over base PatchTST)
- **Result**: AUROC 0.8414, AP 0.2340 (best at epoch 4, declined after)
- **Training**: Full fine-tuning, 11 epochs (hit 3-day wall time)
- **Config**: `jobs/baselines/configs/supervised_patchtst_multiscale_hypotension_full.yaml`
- **Model classes**: `MultiScaleTokenizer`, `_LocalTSTBlock` in `physiojepa/tokenizers.py`

**Design choices for non-overlapping patches:**
- At coarse level (1s), overlap would quadruple attention cost for minimal benefit
- At fine level (200ms), the dual-path fusion already mitigates boundary effects
- RoPE positional encoding assumes distinct temporal positions per token
- Adjacent overlapping patches are largely redundant

## Interpretation

### Why Self-Supervised Probes Work (AUROC 0.830–0.843)

Based on representation analysis findings:

1. The probe learns which patients are high-risk during training (patient
   fingerprint → risk level association)
2. Within high-risk patients, it detects trajectory shifts toward
   pre-hypotensive embedding regions

It does **not** detect a universal pre-hypotensive waveform pattern that
generalizes across patients. The cross-patient k-NN AUROC is only 0.57
(near chance), while within-patient distance ratios show clear separation
(0.75–0.78).

### Multi-scale PatchTST: Why It Underperformed

Despite adding fine-grained 200ms tokenization (beat-level detail), the
multi-scale model (0.841) underperformed standard supervised PatchTST (0.869).

Possible explanations:
- Best performance at epoch 4 suggests overfitting with the additional
  parameters
- The 30-min window already captures beat-level information via the 1s patches
  (each containing ~1 heartbeat at typical HR)
- Additional architecture complexity without additional training data

## Design Decisions

### Embedding Caching vs. Pre-extraction

An alternative approach of pre-extracting all frozen-encoder embeddings to disk
was explored but abandoned due to storage constraints: ~1M samples at shape
`[3, 512, 1800]` in float16 would require approximately 5.5 TB. This far
exceeds available scratch space.

The pragmatic solution was SSD caching of raw Zarr containers (~510 GB total),
allowing on-the-fly encoding during training with the 8.5× I/O speedup from
local NVMe over GPFS. This trades repeated GPU compute for feasible storage
requirements and avoids the need to re-extract embeddings when experimenting
with different encoder checkpoints.
